## Accurate historical outcomes used in the game

**Sinai:** Israeli military forces and civilians were to withdraw behind the international boundary, restoring Egyptian sovereignty. Removal of Israeli settlements was a major issue during the Camp David negotiations.

**Timing:** Annex I of the 1979 treaty provided an interim withdrawal within nine months of the exchange of ratification instruments and completion of withdrawal within three years. Israel completed the main Sinai withdrawal in April 1982. The Taba border area remained disputed until 1989.

**Security:** The treaty included limited force zones, provisions for monitoring and rights of navigation through the Suez Canal and Straits of Tiran. The actual international monitoring arrangements evolved after the treaty, including the Multinational Force and Observers.

**Normalisation:** The treaty ended the state of war and provided for normal diplomatic, economic and cultural relations. The related diplomatic correspondence linked exchange of ambassadors to the interim withdrawal.

**Wider framework:** The September 1978 accords also envisaged negotiations over transitional Palestinian self government in the West Bank and Gaza. They did not determine Palestinian statehood or final status.

**Leadership:** Carter used separate meetings and drafts to overcome repeated deadlock. The three leaders agreed frameworks on 17 September 1978. Sadat and Begin signed the peace treaty on 26 March 1979 with Carter as witness.

## What is fictional or simplified

* All choices other than the main historical choices are hypothetical and are never presented as real treaty offers.
* Confidential briefing cards summarise major government interests and not actual classified instructions.
* Israel, Egypt and trust scores are wholly fictional.
* Carter's two interventions, the seven or eight minute countdown and simplified computer negotiator exist to make the lesson playable.
* The simulation groups some questions from the 1978 frameworks and 1979 final treaty into five rounds, without implying that these five decisions were made together at one meeting.
